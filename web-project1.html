Overview

AI Explorer is an interactive educational website that makes AI fundamentals simple and engaging for beginners. I led the project from user research and Figma prototyping through to frontend development with HTML, CSS, JavaScript and GSAP.

Problem

AI and machine learning concepts are complex and often presented in text-heavy formats, making them difficult and unengaging for beginners to understand.

Here’s how I got there

Discovery

Engagement is one of the most important factor when learning. Visuals and sounds are not only fun, but they dramatically improve learning capacity

Understanding this concept, I’ve implemented 5 ways the engagement could be improved:-

  • Visual Storytelling
  • Ambient sounds
  • Aesthetic animations conveying a story
  • Micro animations
  • No usage of Jargons

Key Insights

Analyzing new learners and AI students

I interviewed new learners and university students who are currently studying AI. I needed to strike a balance between beginner and advanced learning levels.

Gamification

Increases engagement

Proposed Solution

A gamified website that is visually very pleasing while being minimalist, focusing on ambience and visual storytelling rather than traditional text-heavy content.

Wireframes

Low-fidelity sketches let us explore different layouts quickly

Chosen UI

I picked Dark and High Contrast because it focuses on engagement through stunning visuals and high contrast lighting, while also targeting to reduce cognitive load because of emphasis by contrast. Ultra minimalist seems like an overly restrained presentation which can reduce engagement for this particular audience and content, like that IBM AI website I saw. Users will learn better if the material is engaging. The dreamy layout poses challenges such as discarding a type of audience who might appreciate a more traditional website that features text. Hence, Dark and High Contrast seems like a perfect middle ground for all of these concerns.

Final Design

High Contrast, High Aesthetics, High Engagement!

Code Prototype↗

I also used AI tools such as Claude and Figma AI for rapid development. My experience in programming helped me identify flaws in the generated code.

What we achieved

  • Visual storytelling makes complex concepts more engaging. I used visual hierarchy, animations and interactions to break down complex AI/ML concepts into more approachable, digestible information.
  • Progressive interaction reduces information overload. New information is revealed progressively through user interaction rather than presenting everything at once. This reduces cognitive load and allows beginners to focus on one concept at a time.
  • Scroll-driven interaction gives users more control over their learning pace. This prevents learners from getting overwhelmed by a large amount of information, and it also helps them to process information in a structured way.

Trade-offs Made

A few things I realized after prototyping with code

  1. Changed the colour of the brain and increased the saturation of the overall website.
    Reason: I felt in the last moments that the design looked dull in terms of colour, hence I increased saturation. This will also create an emphasis by contrast on the brain.
  2. Removed a story element: the three wires connecting parts of ML to GenAI.
    Reason: The user feedback revealed that the persistent motion created visual clutter on smaller screens, distracting from the primary content.

Reflection

What’s Next?

Next, I want to add subtle sound effects to make the website feel more interactive, since my research suggested that sound improves engagement.

I also want to add more gamified interactions, as research showed that gamification is valued highly among users.

Lessons Learned

  • Prototyping is really important because often what looks good on Figma doesn’t translate well on a real screen. It allows us to fix these problems before final deployment.
  • I should always assume that there might be tradeoffs that I’d have to do in the final version. Hence, I should design pragmatically by not placing too much emphasis on one interaction/element.
  • Always test my prototype and make necessary changes on each step of the process.
