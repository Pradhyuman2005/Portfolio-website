Overview

Sustainability Planner App is a user-centric mobile application that bridges the gap between environmental awareness and everyday practicality, helping shoppers save money, reduce waste, and avoid duplicate purchases in the aisle. Grounded in competitive audits, user interviews, and survey data, I led the end-to-end UX process from affinity mapping and persona creation to iterative Figma prototyping and A/B testing. Through strategic pivots like supermarket-only geofencing and refined visual hierarchy, the final design balances an encouraging Sustainability Score dashboard with fast barcode scanning and accessible, affordable product swaps.

Problem

Everyday shoppers want to live sustainably, yet existing eco-apps fail mainstream users by prioritizing rigid green rules over budgets, time, and convenience. Busy consumers lack a simple, trustworthy tool to avoid impulse overspending and make affordable, eco-friendly choices directly in the aisle.

Here’s how I got there

Discovery

60% of shoppers prioritize price over eco-labels, while 70% cite time and inconvenience as major barriers to sustainable living. Existing green apps fail mainstream users by prioritizing rigid sustainability rules over everyday convenience and budgets.

To bridge this gap, I led an end-to-end UX research and iterative design process to build a non-intrusive, budget-friendly sustainability planner tailored for real-world shopping habits:

  • Competitive Audit: Evaluated four existing platforms (Olio, Too Good To Go, Yuka, and JouleBug!) to identify UI strengths and user-centric gaps.
  • Mixed-Methods Research: Conducted five in-depth interviews and a ten-person survey, synthesizing findings through affinity mapping.
  • Personas & Storyboards: Defined three core user archetypes and mapped in-aisle shopping scenarios to guide feature decisions.
  • Strategic Ideation: Replaced intrusive background tracking with supermarket-only geofencing and pivoted away from complex AR to ensure accessibility for all ages.
  • A/B Testing & Iteration: Tested two distinct Figma prototypes with five users to refine visual hierarchy, remove false affordances, and optimize tap targets.

A user-centric sustainability planner built around real-world grocery shopping habits. What I achieved

  • Aligned Sustainability with Personal Savings: Balanced environmental goals with financial incentives by pairing a central Sustainability Score dashboard with instant barcode alerts for duplicate purchases and cheaper, eco-friendly swaps.
  • Built Trust Through Privacy and Simplicity: Replaced intrusive tracking with supermarket-only geofencing and eliminated jargon, complex AR, and false affordances so users of all tech comfort levels can navigate effortlessly.
  • Designed for Inclusive Accessibility: Integrated high-padding, easy-to-tap controls supporting dyslexia-friendly fonts, higher contrast, larger text, and reduced animations.

Reflection

What’s Next?

Expand the recommendation engine to incorporate personalized dietary restrictions, allergies, and household pantry expiration tracking so sustainable swaps remain hyper-relevant to everyday routines.

Lessons Learned

  • Separate primary and secondary goals: Early screens prioritized money saved, overshadowing the core mission. Elevating the Sustainability Score to the main focus kept sustainability central while positioning financial savings as a supportive incentive.
  • Familiar patterns beat visual trends: Trendy neomorphism and decorative lines confused testers and felt cold. Switching to clean layouts, iOS-style toggles, and clear shadows removed guesswork and built trust.
  • Prioritize accessibility over flashy features: Exciting ideas like AR reuse scanning risked overwhelming older, less tech-savvy users. I learned to drop complex features and pivot toward simple, inclusive workflows.
